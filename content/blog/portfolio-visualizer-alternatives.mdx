Choosing a portfolio research tool starts with the questions you want to answer.
This starter outline offers a framework for evaluating Portfolio Visualizer
alternatives and planning your own comparisons.

## Start with your research question

Before comparing tools, write down the portfolio, contribution schedule, and time
horizon you want to study. A useful comparison holds these inputs constant.

- Which holdings and weights will you use?
- Are you studying an initial investment, recurring contributions, or both?
- Which benchmark helps you interpret the result?

## What to compare in a backtesting tool

### Data coverage and assumptions

Check the available markets, historical range, and treatment of dividends,
corporate actions, and missing observations. Record each tool's assumptions before
interpreting differences in its output.

### Portfolio and contribution controls

Look for inputs that match your question: allocation weights, starting capital,
contribution timing, and any rebalancing rules you need to model.

### Results you can interpret

Consider how clearly each tool presents its equity curve, benchmark, drawdowns,
and contributions. Separate money added to a portfolio from changes in its value.

## A comparison workflow to build on

1. Define a simple baseline with one holding, such as QQQ.
2. Use the same date range and starting investment in each tool.
3. Document differences in data and calculation assumptions.
4. Add recurring contributions or multiple holdings one change at a time.
5. Save your observations alongside the inputs that produced them.
